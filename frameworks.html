Frameworks & tools

Each framework starts from one question and ends in something you can check: a paper with the evidence and, where it helps, a tool you can run in the browser. They run from the scale of a country down to a single trip.

Fiscal Infrastructure LadderCountries

Is Singapore’s reserve income a fiscal multiplier, or infrastructure?

From 1994 to 2023, Singapore’s real GDP, the Net Investment Returns Contribution from its reserves, and its population move together in the long run, and about 55% of any deviation is corrected within a year. The ladder reads that pattern as fiscal infrastructure rather than a fiscal multiplier, and gives a lens for comparing how other states build the same institutions.

Concentric Value ModelCompanies

Which companies are worth owning when most of their value is intangible?

Graham’s value investing was built for tangible assets and quieter markets. The model adapts it to companies whose value sits in intangibles and whose prices are shaped by options activity, with ten dimensions arranged in six layers. Tested on 200 large caps from 2016 to 2025, its sector-allocation rules beat an equal-weight benchmark, while picking individual stocks mainly helped during COVID-19. It works best as a sector-quality classifier.

The tool scores a listed company across the ten dimensions and returns a composite score, a Graham quartile, and bear, base and bull scenarios.

Tokenized Asset Valuation EngineAssets

What does tokenizing an asset do to its cost of capital?

Tokenizing an asset can change a firm’s cost of capital in several ways at once. TAVE separates five channels in a weighted-average-cost-of-capital model, liquidity among them, and tests the framework with an event study of tokenization announcements and practitioner evidence.

The tool shows how the modeled cost of capital and enterprise value respond to the inputs you set: book value, share tokenized, token liquidity, jurisdiction and blockchain. It is a research instrument, not a valuation or advice.

Core Integrity Adherence–Outcome FrameworkEnterprise systems

Does architectural discipline show up in a firm’s results?

Enterprise platforms come with advice: keep the core system unmodified and integrate through governed interfaces. The framework asks whether following that advice shows up in performance, using a panel of 167 large US filers from 2019 to 2025. The evidence does not support a general rule. The right level of discipline is firm-specific, and the paper maps the limits of what pooled public data can show.

Because no universal rule holds, the framework ends in a decision matrix rather than a benchmark. It places each business capability by its business impact and by how well the standard product fits it, and sets the treatment, from “standard, zero deviation” to “extend beside the core”. A regulatory obligation overrides the quadrant.

Governance Observability LadderGovernance and AI

Can enterprise-architecture governance be measured from what firms disclose?

Most claims about governance rest on what firms choose to say. The EAGI paper tests that across 950 firm-years of 167 large US filers from 2019 to 2025. An index built from voluntary annual-report text fails as a measure, while the same method works on AI vocabulary, so the failure lies in the construct rather than the technique. What can be observed depends on what regulation compels and auditors attest. The ladder orders five public sources on that basis, from voluntary narrative to attested control failures, and sets five tests a source must pass before it is used.

The forward-looking part concerns AI. Across firms, a more disciplined core, meaning a standard core with harmonized data and governed interfaces, predicts more AI adoption disclosed the following year. Governance disclosure does not. The result is exploratory and still to be re-tested on new data, and the paper does not show that AI pays more on a disciplined core.

The TRVA Navigator applies the ladder and the Core Integrity matrix to an enterprise’s own situation. It runs entirely in the browser, and nothing you enter leaves the page.

FlightCompassTrips

What will this trip really cost?

FlightCompass compares the ways to pay for the same journey: cash fares, miles, cashback portals, currency-based booking and premium-card hotel programs, across more than 60 origin hubs and 100 destinations. It marks the cheapest airport on a world map and scores each strategy out of 100 for every route.
