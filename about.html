Background

I am a German industrial engineer living and working in Southeast Asia – currently based in Thailand, building at the intersection of enterprise technology, digital finance, and human performance.

By day I drive progress at SAP, helping Fortune Global 500 companies navigate digital transformation across APAC – designing enterprise architecture, guiding cloud migrations, and translating technical complexity into strategic clarity.

By research I am completing a DBA examining how blockchain infrastructure is reshaping digital finance in Asia, with Singapore at the center – a question I think matters enormously for every large enterprise operating in this region over the next decade.

By practice I am obsessed with the long game – nearly seven years of daily meditation, a decade and a half of consistent training, and a genuine belief that how you build your body and mind is inseparable from how you build your career and life.

What ties it together is how I work. I think like an architect, in structures rather than tasks, and I connect business and technology, Europe and Asia, practice and research. I play the long game. I have never been afraid to leave the familiar behind when it was time to grow. And I keep an eye for the detail others walk past.

I have lived in three countries, built bridges across three continents, and traveled to 42 nations. I photograph what I find along the way.

This site is where those passions come together – professional thinking, personal frameworks, photography, and an ongoing attempt to create a life that is both high-performing and genuinely worth living.

Longevity pyramid

Some similarities exist to Maslow’s hierarchy of needs. My structure focuses more on increasing fulfillment, happiness, and peace.

  1. Purpose

    Living a purposeful life with passion might be one of the most fulfilling achievements to strive for.

  2. Relationships

    • Family
    • Friends
    • Relationships
    • Community
    • Mentors

    There is this saying that you are the sum of the five people you spend the most time with. I don’t know whether it is true, but choose carefully and always be open to learn and be mentored.

  3. Habits

    • Sleep
    • Nutrition and supplements
    • Hydration
    • Strength training
    • Cardio
    • High-intensity training
    • Yoga
    • Mobility and balance
    • Meditation
    • Journaling
    • Learning
    • Resting
    • Being productive and hard-working
    • Reading
    • Volunteering
    • Time in nature
    • Morning sunlight
    • Sauna
    • Cold exposure 6+ hours after strength training
    • Intermittent and dopamine fasting
    • Coffee
    • Preventive check-ups and blood work
    • and more

    What you do every day is vital and largely determines who you are. Hence, good habits are essential. Start small and improve continuously. What you reap is what you sow. Cut out unhealthy dopamine spikes and rather focus on delayed gratification while still having fun.

  4. Focus

    Dreams, priorities and goals:

    • Education
    • Spirituality
    • Health
    • Wealth
    • Career
    • Emotions
    • Hobbies
    • Travel
    • Leisure
    • Adventure

    Become clear regarding what you want in life. Write down goals in each area and prioritize. In my opinion being well educated, even untraditionally, is crucial – technology democratizes opportunity, so hone your skills. Also, everybody worships something; make sure you know what that is for you and whether it’s serving you.

  5. Values

    • Love
    • Gratitude
    • Growth
    • and more

    Make sure you know your values. They are the foundation, which influence your thoughts, words, and actions. They make you unique and give you strength.
